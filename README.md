## Course Overview 

This three-day short course will provide advanced, practical training in Earth Observation (EO), remote sensing, field data collection and geospatial analysis for coastal monitoring and disaster impact applications in Pacific Island contexts. The course follows the preliminary agenda, moving from an overview of EO and multi-temporal sensors in Digital Earth Pacific, GitHub and Digital Earth Pacific account setup, introductory Python activities, and QField preparation, to field data collection for landcover and marine habitat mapping, a coastal field survey along, post-processing and incorporation of field data into Digital Earth Pacific, machine learning classification workflows, change detection and statistics, and student group presentations. 

## Rationale 

The course is designed for mature and final-year geospatial students at USP and responds to practical training needs in EO, GIS, programming, field-based data collection, data post-processing and applied analysis. It builds on the GRSS-IEEE, GEO-CRADLE and Digital Earth Pacific partnership with regional universities and addresses priority Pacific applications related to coastal monitoring, disaster impacts, landcover mapping and marine habitat mapping.  

The course is deliberately practice-oriented: students will set up the required platforms, undertake laboratory exercises, collect field data using QField, process and incorporate field observations into Digital Earth Pacific, apply machine learning classification workflows, analyse change detection outputs and present their applied findings. 

## Learning Objectives 

- Explain the role of EO, multi-temporal sensors and Digital Earth Pacific in coastal monitoring and disaster impact assessment. 
- Set up digital tools for the course, including GitHub, Digital Earth Pacific and QField. 
- Apply introductory Python activities to support EO and geospatial analysis workflows. 
- Collect field data for landcover and marine habitat mapping using QField, including field testing around USP and a coastal field survey. 
- Post-process field data and incorporate field observations into Digital Earth Pacific. 
- Describe classifiers and machine learning concepts and apply a machine learning classification workflow. 
- Generate and interpret change detection and statistics outputs relevant to coastal monitoring, landcover mapping and disaster impact applications. 
- Present applied findings through group presentations and reporting back. 

## Delivery Approach 
The course will be delivered through a structured mix of lectures, laboratory sessions, field activities, daily quizzes, recap activities, evaluation forms and group presentations. The three-day delivery sequence is designed to build progressively from orientation and account setup, to field data collection and processing, and finally to machine learning classification, change detection, statistics and student reporting.

## Participation
The expected participants are approximately 30 – 40 USP students, primarily 300-level and postgraduate students in geospatial, environmental and related disciplines. The course is intended to support both academic learning and professional readiness by exposing students to applied EO workflows relevant to Pacific Island conditions. 

## Expected Outcomes 
- Students understand how EO, multi-temporal sensors and Digital Earth Pacific can support coastal monitoring and disaster impact assessment. 
- Students have practical experience setting up and using GitHub, Digital Earth Pacific and QField for EO training activities. 
- Students complete introductory Python, field data collection, post-processing, machine learning classification and change detection activities. 
- Students generate and interpret applied outputs related to landcover, marine habitat mapping, coastal monitoring, disaster impacts and change statistics. 
- Students present their applied findings through group presentations and complete the course evaluation process.

## Project: EO-Based GRSS Training for PICTS- Description 
The project is about coordination of an EO-based GRSS education program dedicated to young scientists and stakeholders in the Pacific Islanding territories supported by GRSS REACT & IADF TCs and Australian Chapters, in cooperation with Regional GEOs (AOGEO and EuroGEO), the GEO Enabling Mechanisms GEO-CRADLE, AI4EO & EO4SDG, and the Digital Earth Pacific. The expected outcome of this project aligned with the GRSS Goals, given that: 

- It will enhance the understanding and the use of EO in practical applications (in the disaster resilience and health domains) which contribute to global innovation. 
- It will create EO curriculum to support life-long learning. 
- It will provide opportunities for career and professional development. 
- It will establish a fruitful collaboration for sharing of knowledge for the benefit of humanity. 
- It will promote the visibility of GRSS and the awareness about the benefits and opportunities that GRSS provides to its membership (such as participation in high-level events and access to publications) on the one hand, and on the other hand it will encourage new members to join and contribute actively to GRSS. 
- It will establish a more concrete and constructive win-win cooperation between GRSS and GEO towards tangible and long-term outcomes (which may be reflected in the signing of a MoU) in support of the relevant GEO Work Program activities where GRSS contributes already (GEO enabling mechanisms GEO-CRADLE, AI4EO & EO4SDG).
